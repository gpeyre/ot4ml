\begin{abstract}
This note isolates one conditional-expectation operator that controls
three aspects of entropic optimal transport: dual curvature, the local
convergence of Sinkhorn's algorithm, and semi-dual conditioning. At the
optimal coupling, the positive full-dual curvature operator is
$\eps^{-1}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&T\\T^\ast&I\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
the Jacobian of one complete Sinkhorn cycle is $T^\ast T$, and exact
elimination of one potential gives the semi-dual Hessian
$\eps^{-1}(I-T^\ast T)$. This common structure yields the local rate,
strict-gap criteria, small-$\eps$ asymptotics, and explicit parameters
for extrapolation and momentum. It also explains why variable
projection and quasi-Newton methods are natural alternatives to long
scaling iterations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Dual Hessian and conditional expectation}

The correct geometry for the dual potentials is not an unweighted
Euclidean space: it is the product of the two marginal $L^2$ spaces. In
this geometry, the Hessian has an especially simple block form.

\paragraph{Entropic primal and dual.}
Let $\Xx$ and $\Yy$ be standard Borel spaces, let
$\al\in\mathcal P(\Xx)$ and $\be\in\mathcal P(\Yy)$, and let
$c:\Xx\times\Yy\to\R\cup\{+\infty\}$ be measurable. The
KL-regularized transport value is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:primal}
  \MK_c^\eps(\al,\be)
  :=
  \inf_{\pi\in\Couplings(\al,\be)}
  \left\{
    \int c\,\dd\pi
    +
    \eps\KL(\pi\,\|\,\al\otimes\be)
  \right\}.
\end{equation}
This is the entropy-regularized transport problem introduced
computationally by Cuturi \cite{Cuturi2013}. Its concave dual objective
is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:dual-energy}
  \Dd_\eps(f,g)
  :=
  \int_{\Xx}f\,\dd\al+\int_{\Yy}g\,\dd\be
  -\eps\int_{\Xx\times\Yy}
  \left[
  \exp\left(\frac{f(x)+g(y)-c(x,y)}{\eps}\right)-1
  \right]
  \dd\al(x)\dd\be(y)
\end{equation}
and $\MK_c^\eps(\al,\be)=\sup_{f,g}\Dd_\eps(f,g)$ under the standard
duality assumptions. Assume henceforth that an optimal pair
$(f^\star,g^\star)$ exists and that its Gibbs coupling
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:optimal-gibbs-plan}
  \dd\pi^\star(x,y)
  =
  \exp\left(\frac{f^\star(x)+g^\star(y)-c(x,y)}{\eps}\right)
  \dd\al(x)\dd\be(y)
\end{equation}
has marginals $(\al,\be)$. The gauge transformation
$(f,g)\mapsto(f+s,g-s)$ leaves \eqref{eq:dual-energy} unchanged. We
isolate the nonconstant correlation modes through the centered spaces
\[
  L_0^2(\al):=\left\{h:\int h\,\dd\al=0\right\},
  \qquad
  L_0^2(\be):=\left\{k:\int k\,\dd\be=0\right\},
\]
endowed with their usual weighted norms. We write
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:centering-projectors}
  \operatorname{cent}_{\al}h:=h-\int h\dd\al,
  \qquad
  \operatorname{cent}_{\be}k:=k-\int k\dd\be
\end{equation}
for the orthogonal centering projectors.
The full quotient by the gauge also retains the common-constant
direction $(\one,\one)$; its curvature will be recorded separately.

\paragraph{Conditional expectation.}
Disintegrating $\pi^\star$ defines
\begin{align}
  T:L^2(\be)&\longrightarrow L^2(\al),
  &Tk(x)&:=\E_{\pi^\star}[k(Y)\mid X=x],
  \label{eq:T-definition}
  \\
  T^\ast:L^2(\al)&\longrightarrow L^2(\be),
  &T^\ast h(y)&:=\E_{\pi^\star}[h(X)\mid Y=y].
  \label{eq:Tstar-definition}
\end{align}
The notation is literal: $\ip{h}{Tk}_{\al}=\ip{T^\ast h}{k}_{\be}$.
Conditional Jensen gives $\opnorm{T}\leq1$, while
$T\one=\one$ and $T^\ast\one=\one$. Hence both operators preserve
centered functions. We use the same symbols for their restrictions and
set
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:sigma-gap}
  \sigma_\eps
  :=
  \opnorm{T:L_0^2(\be)\to L_0^2(\al)},
  \qquad
  \gamma_\eps:=1-\sigma_\eps^2.
\end{equation}
Thus $\sigma_\eps$ is the maximal nonconstant correlation carried by
$\pi^\star$, while $\gamma_\eps$ is the spectral gap of the Markov
operator $T^\ast T$ on $L_0^2(\be)$.

\begin{proposition}[Full dual Hessian]
  \label{prop:full-dual-hessian}
  In $L^2(\al)\oplus L^2(\be)$, the positive curvature operator of the
  concave dual at $(f^\star,g^\star)$ is
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:full-dual-hessian}
    H_{\mathrm{dual}}
    =
    -\nabla^2\Dd_\eps(f^\star,g^\star)
    =
    \frac1\eps
    \begin{pmatrix}
      I&T\\
      T^\ast&I
    \end{pmatrix}.
  \end{equation}
  Equivalently,
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:full-dual-quadratic-form}
    -\eps\,D^2\Dd_\eps(f^\star,g^\star)[(h,k),(h,k)]
    =
    \E_{\pi^\star}\bigl[(h(X)+k(Y))^2\bigr].
  \end{equation}
  On $L_0^2(\al)\oplus L_0^2(\be)$,
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:full-dual-curvature}
    \frac{1-\sigma_\eps}{\eps}I
    \preceq H_{\mathrm{dual}}
    \preceq
    \frac{1+\sigma_\eps}{\eps}I,
    \qquad
    \kappa_{\mathrm{dual},0}
    =
    \frac{1+\sigma_\eps}{1-\sigma_\eps}.
  \end{equation}
  On the full space modulo the gauge direction $(\one,-\one)$, the
  additional eigenvalue is $2/\eps$, and hence
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:full-dual-quotient-condition}
    \kappa_{\mathrm{dual}}
    =
    \frac{2}{1-\sigma_\eps}.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Twice differentiating $-\Dd_\eps$ gives
$\eps^{-1}\E_{\pi^\star}[(h(X)+k(Y))^2]$. Expanding the square and using
\eqref{eq:T-definition} gives \eqref{eq:full-dual-hessian}. On a
singular pair $Tv=s u$, $T^\ast u=s v$, the two vectors $(u,\pm v)$
have eigenvalues $(1\pm s)/\eps$. Taking the extremal centered singular
value $s=\sigma_\eps$ proves \eqref{eq:full-dual-curvature}. The omitted
constant gauge vector $(\one,-\one)$ has eigenvalue zero, whereas
$(\one,\one)$ has eigenvalue $2/\eps$. This proves
\eqref{eq:full-dual-quotient-condition}.
\end{proof}

For discrete marginals $\al=\sum_i \mathrm a_i\delta_{x_i}$ and
$\be=\sum_j \mathrm b_j\delta_{y_j}$, with optimal coupling matrix
$\mathrm P^\star$, the same operators read
\[
  Tk=\mathrm D_{\mathrm a}^{-1}\mathrm P^\star k,
  \qquad
  T^\ast h=\mathrm D_{\mathrm b}^{-1}
  (\mathrm P^\star)^{\mathsf T}h,
\]
where $\mathrm D_{\mathrm a}=\operatorname{diag}(\mathrm a)$ and
$\mathrm D_{\mathrm b}=\operatorname{diag}(\mathrm b)$. These formulas
are adjoint in the $\mathrm a$- and $\mathrm b$-weighted inner products,
not in the unweighted Euclidean ones.

\section{Sinkhorn Jacobian and local rate}

A full Sinkhorn cycle is a nonlinear map on one potential. Its
linearization at the fixed point is exactly the two-step conditional
expectation $T^\ast T$, so the local convergence question reduces to a
Markov spectral gap.

\paragraph{Soft transforms and the cycle map.}
Exact block maximization of $\Dd_\eps$ is written with the continuous
soft $c$-transforms
\begin{align}
  g^{\bar c,\eps}(x)
  &:=-\eps\log\int_{\Yy}
  \exp\left(\frac{g(y)-c(x,y)}{\eps}\right)\dd\be(y),
  \label{eq:soft-transform-g}
  \\
  f^{c,\eps}(y)
  &:=-\eps\log\int_{\Xx}
  \exp\left(\frac{f(x)-c(x,y)}{\eps}\right)\dd\al(x).
  \label{eq:soft-transform-f}
\end{align}
One complete cycle on the target potential is therefore
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:sinkhorn-cycle}
  \Ss_\eps(g):=(g^{\bar c,\eps})^{c,\eps},
  \qquad
  g^{(\ell+1)}=\Ss_\eps(g^{(\ell)}).
\end{equation}
The opposite ordering acts on $L^2(\al)$ and has the same nonzero
spectrum.

\begin{proposition}[Jacobian and exact local Sinkhorn rate]
  \label{prop:sinkhorn-local-rate}
  Assume that the two soft transforms are twice differentiable near
  the optimum. Then
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:coordinate-jacobians}
    D[g\mapsto g^{\bar c,\eps}](g^\star)=-T,
    \qquad
    D[f\mapsto f^{c,\eps}](f^\star)=-T^\ast,
    \qquad
    D\Ss_\eps(g^\star)=T^\ast T.
  \end{equation}
  Consequently, for the centered error
  $e^{(\ell)}=\operatorname{cent}_{\be}(g^{(\ell)}-g^\star)$ of any Sinkhorn sequence
  converging to $g^\star$ modulo constants,
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:local-error-recursion}
    e^{(\ell+1)}
    =
    T^\ast T e^{(\ell)}+o(\norm{e^{(\ell)}}_\be),
    \qquad
    \limsup_{\ell\to\infty}
    \frac{\norm{e^{(\ell+1)}}_\be}
         {\norm{e^{(\ell)}}_\be}
    \leq \sigma_\eps^2.
  \end{equation}
  If $\sigma_\eps^2$ is an isolated eigenvalue and the asymptotic error
  has a nonzero projection onto its eigenspace, the ratio converges to
  $\sigma_\eps^2$.
  Moreover,
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:conditional-poincare-gap}
    \gamma_\eps
    =
    \inf_{\substack{k\in L_0^2(\be)\\\norm{k}_\be=1}}
    \E_{\pi^\star}\left[\Var(k(Y)\mid X)\right].
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiating the log-partition maps
\eqref{eq:soft-transform-g}--\eqref{eq:soft-transform-f} at the optimal
Gibbs coupling gives the first two identities in
\eqref{eq:coordinate-jacobians}; the
chain rule gives the third. Taylor expansion proves
\eqref{eq:local-error-recursion}. Finally, for centered $k$,
\[
  \ip{k}{(I-T^\ast T)k}_\be
  =
  \norm{k}_\be^2-\norm{Tk}_\al^2
  =
  \E_{\pi^\star}[\Var(k(Y)\mid X)].
\]
Taking the infimum over the unit sphere gives
\eqref{eq:conditional-poincare-gap}.
\end{proof}

The reverse cycle $f\mapsto(f^{c,\eps})^{\bar c,\eps}$ has Jacobian
$TT^\ast$ on
$L_0^2(\al)$. Since $T^\ast T$ and $TT^\ast$ have the same nonzero
spectrum, either orientation has the same local rate. In finite
spaces, this recovers the classical second-singular-value formula for
matrix scaling \cite{Knight2008,LehmannEtAl2022}.

\paragraph{When is the rate strictly smaller than one?}
Positivity of the Gibbs kernel is enough in finite dimension but not on
an arbitrary infinite space: centered approximate modes may retain
correlation arbitrarily close to one. The following input-level bound
is simple and explicit.

Define the cost oscillation modulo separable terms by
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:separable-cost-oscillation}
  \Omega_c
  :=
  \inf_{\xi,\zeta}
  \operatorname{ess\,osc}_{\al\otimes\be}
  \bigl(c(x,y)-\xi(x)-\zeta(y)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Here the infimum is over measurable real-valued functions
$\xi$ on $\Xx$ and $\zeta$ on $\Yy$.
For compact supports and continuous $c$, also define the invariant
four-point oscillation
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:four-point-cost-oscillation}
  \Delta_c
  :=
  \sup_{x,x',y,y'}
  \bigl[c(x,y')+c(x',y)-c(x,y)-c(x',y')\bigr],
\end{equation}
where the supremum is over the product of the two supports.

\begin{proposition}[Explicit strict-gap criteria]
  \label{prop:strict-gap}
  If $\Omega_c<\infty$, then
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:doeblin-rate-bound}
    \sigma_\eps
    \leq
    1-e^{-2\Omega_c/\eps}
    <1,
    \qquad
    \sigma_\eps^2\leq\bigl(1-e^{-2\Omega_c/\eps}\bigr)^2.
  \end{equation}
  If the supports are compact and $c$ is continuous, then the
  projective bound also holds:
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:birkhoff-local-bound}
    \sigma_\eps
    \leq
    \Lambda_\eps
    :=
    \tanh\left(\frac{\Delta_c}{4\eps}\right)
    <1.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Adding $\xi(x)+\zeta(y)$ to $c$ only changes the dual potentials. For any
$\eta>0$, choose such terms so that the resulting cost has essential
oscillation at most $\Omega_c+\eta$. Write
$k_\eps=e^{-c/\eps}$, $m=\essinf k_\eps$, $M=\esssup k_\eps$, and
$\dd\pi^\star=u(x)k_\eps(x,y)v(y)\dd\al\dd\be$. The two marginal equations
give
\[
  \frac1{MV}\leq u\leq\frac1{mV},
  \qquad
  \frac1{MU}\leq v\leq\frac1{mU},
  \qquad
  \frac1M\leq UV\leq\frac1m,
\]
where $U=\int u\dd\al$ and $V=\int v\dd\be$. Hence the density
$r=\dd\pi^\star/\dd(\al\otimes\be)$ satisfies
$r\geq(m/M)^2\geq e^{-2(\Omega_c+\eta)/\eps}=: \delta_\eta$. Decomposing
$r=\delta_\eta+(1-\delta_\eta)\widetilde r$ shows that, on centered
functions, $T=(1-\delta_\eta)\widetilde T$ with
$\opnorm{\widetilde T}\leq1$. Letting $\eta\downarrow0$ proves
\eqref{eq:doeblin-rate-bound}.

Under compactness, the conditional kernel is continuous and $T$ is
compact. Birkhoff's theorem bounds both half-cycles in Hilbert's
projective metric by $\Lambda_\eps$ \cite{FranklinLorenz1989}. If
$\sigma_\eps>0$, compactness provides a continuous centered singular
pair $Tv=\sigma_\eps u$, $T^\ast u=\sigma_\eps v$. Differentiating the
two projective bounds gives
$\sigma_\eps\operatorname{osc}(u)\leq
\Lambda_\eps\operatorname{osc}(v)$ and the reverse inequality with
$u,v$ exchanged. Multiplication yields
$\sigma_\eps^2\leq\Lambda_\eps^2$; the case $\sigma_\eps=0$ is immediate.
\end{proof}

On unbounded spaces, cost growth must be balanced against marginal
tails. Conforti, Durmus, and Greco prove quantitative contraction for
quadratic costs under asymptotically positive log-concavity profiles
\cite{ConfortiDurmusGreco2026}; Eckstein obtains weighted Hilbert
contraction under compatible cost-growth and tail assumptions
\cite{Eckstein2025}. These are useful input-level criteria, but their
norms should not be identified automatically with the exact $L^2$
gap \eqref{eq:conditional-poincare-gap}.

\paragraph{Why a linear small-$\eps$ gap is natural.}
In a smooth connected Monge regime, the conditional law of $Y$ given
$X=x$ has width $O(\sqrt\eps)$ around the hard transport
map. The conditional variance in
\eqref{eq:conditional-poincare-gap} is therefore typically of order
$\eps$, leading to $\sigma_\eps^2=1-C\eps+o(\eps)$.

A precise conditional statement is as follows. Assume that $\Xx$ and $\Yy$
are smooth connected $d$-dimensional manifolds, that the marginals have
positive smooth densities, and that hard transport is induced by a
smooth diffeomorphism $\mathsf M:\Xx\to\Yy$. Let $g_0^\star$ be the hard
target potential and set
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:monge-transverse-hessian}
  H(x)
  :=
  D_{yy}^2\bigl(c(x,\cdot)-g_0^\star\bigr)(\mathsf M(x)),
  \qquad
  A(y):=H(\mathsf M^{-1}(y))^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Suppose that $H$ is uniformly positive and that the uniform Laplace
expansion of the entropic conditionals permits passage to the first
nonzero Rayleigh quotient, for instance through Mosco convergence of
the rescaled forms and compact convergence of their resolvents. Define
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:limiting-poincare-eigenvalue}
  \lambda_1(A,\be)
  :=
  \inf_{\substack{\int k\dd\be=0\\\int k^2\dd\be=1}}
  \int_{\Yy}\ip{A(y)\nabla k(y)}{\nabla k(y)}\dd\be(y).
\end{equation}
Uniform ellipticity and a Poincar\'e inequality for $\be$ ensure that
$\lambda_1(A,\be)>0$.

\begin{proposition}[Smooth small-regularization law]
  \label{prop:smooth-small-epsilon}
  Under the preceding assumptions,
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:smooth-small-epsilon-rate}
    \gamma_\eps
    =
    \eps\lambda_1(A,\be)+o(\eps),
    \qquad
    \sigma_\eps^2
    =
    1-\eps\lambda_1(A,\be)+o(\eps),
    \qquad
    \sigma_\eps
    =
    1-\frac\eps2\lambda_1(A,\be)+o(\eps).
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Writing $\pi_\eps^\star$ for the optimal coupling at regularization
$\eps$, the uniform Laplace expansion of $Y\mid X=x$ gives
$\Cov(Y\mid X=x)=\eps H(x)^{-1}+o(\eps)$. Hence, for smooth $k$,
\[
  \frac1\eps
  \E_{\pi_\eps^\star}[\Var(k(Y)\mid X)]
  \longrightarrow
  \int_{\Xx}
  \ip{H(x)^{-1}\nabla k(\mathsf M(x))}
     {\nabla k(\mathsf M(x))}\dd\al(x).
\]
Since $\mathsf M_\#\al=\be$, the right-hand side is the form in
\eqref{eq:limiting-poincare-eigenvalue}. Spectral convergence permits
passage to the first Rayleigh quotient in
\eqref{eq:conditional-poincare-gap}, giving the first expansion. The
other two follow from $\gamma_\eps=1-\sigma_\eps^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Exact Gaussian benchmark]
  \label{ex:gaussian-benchmark}
  For $\al=\be=\Gaussian(0,1)$ and
  $c(x,y)=\frac12(x-y)^2$, the optimal coupling is Gaussian with
  correlation
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:gaussian-correlation}
    r_\eps
    =
    \frac{\sqrt{\eps^2+4}-\eps}{2}.
  \end{equation}
  Hermite polynomials diagonalize $T$, so $\sigma_\eps=r_\eps$.
  Thus $\sigma_\eps=1-\eps/2+O(\eps^2)$ and
  $\sigma_\eps^2=1-\eps+O(\eps^2)$. This is
  Proposition~\ref{prop:smooth-small-epsilon} with
  $A=1$ and $\lambda_1(A,\be)=1$.
\end{example}

\paragraph{Local $L^2$ versus global Hilbert contraction.}
For $\Delta_c>0$,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:hilbert-small-epsilon}
  1-\Lambda_\eps^2
  \sim
  4e^{-\Delta_c/(2\eps)}
  \qquad(\eps\downarrow0).
\end{equation}
This is exponentially more pessimistic than the smooth local law
$\gamma_\eps\sim\eps\lambda_1(A,\be)$. There is no contradiction.
Hilbert's projective metric gives a global guarantee over the full positive cone and uses
no smoothness of the iterates. The $L^2$ result is local to the optimal
coupling and exploits the regular diffusive structure of the dominant
modes. Chizat, Delalande, and Va\v{s}kevi\v{c}ius similarly obtain
polynomial rather than universal exponential dependences under
continuous regularity and log-concavity assumptions
\cite{ChizatDelalandeVaskevicius2026}.

\section{Extrapolation and momentum}
\label{sec:acceleration}

Once the local spectrum is reduced to
$\operatorname{spec}(T^\ast T)\subset[0,\sigma_\eps^2]$, standard
polynomial acceleration applies. This section distinguishes scalar extrapolation,
which improves the gap by a constant factor, from momentum, which gives
the characteristic square-root improvement.

\paragraph{Scalar extrapolation.}
Consider the relaxed full cycle
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:extrapolated-cycle}
  g^{(\ell+1)}
  =
  g^{(\ell)}
  +
  \omega\bigl(\Ss_\eps(g^{(\ell)})-g^{(\ell)}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Its centered Jacobian is
$I-\omega(I-T^\ast T)$.

\begin{proposition}[Optimal scalar extrapolation]
  \label{prop:optimal-extrapolation}
  If $\gamma_\eps>0$, the choice
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:optimal-extrapolation-parameter}
    \omega_{\mathrm{ext}}
    =
    \frac{2}{1+\gamma_\eps}
  \end{equation}
  minimizes the worst local factor of
  \eqref{eq:extrapolated-cycle} over the spectral interval
  $[0,1-\gamma_\eps]$. The resulting factor is
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:optimal-extrapolation-rate}
    r_{\mathrm{ext}}
    =
    \frac{1-\gamma_\eps}{1+\gamma_\eps}
    =
    \frac{\sigma_\eps^2}{2-\sigma_\eps^2},
  \end{equation}
  compared with $\sigma_\eps^2=1-\gamma_\eps$ for ordinary Sinkhorn.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On an eigenmode $T^\ast T k=\lambda k$, the factor is
$1-\omega(1-\lambda)$, with $1-\lambda\in[\gamma_\eps,1]$. The minimax
choice balances the two endpoint values:
$1-\omega\gamma_\eps=-(1-\omega)$. Solving gives
\eqref{eq:optimal-extrapolation-parameter} and
\eqref{eq:optimal-extrapolation-rate}.
\end{proof}

For $\gamma_\eps\ll1$, scalar extrapolation changes
$1-\gamma_\eps$ into
$1-2\gamma_\eps+O(\gamma_\eps^2)$. It is useful but does not change the
scaling of the iteration count.

\paragraph{Momentum and square-root acceleration.}
Add one memory term to the extrapolated cycle:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:momentum-cycle}
  g^{(\ell+1)}
  =
  g^{(\ell)}
  +
  \omega\bigl(\Ss_\eps(g^{(\ell)})-g^{(\ell)}\bigr)
  +
  \vartheta\bigl(g^{(\ell)}-g^{(\ell-1)}\bigr).
\end{equation}
This is the heavy-ball polynomial applied locally to
$I-T^\ast T$ \cite{Polyak1964}.

\begin{proposition}[Optimal local momentum parameters]
  \label{prop:optimal-momentum}
  For a known gap $\gamma_\eps>0$, choose
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:optimal-momentum-parameters}
    \omega_{\mathrm{mom}}
    =
    \frac{4}{(1+\sqrt{\gamma_\eps})^2},
    \qquad
    \vartheta_{\mathrm{mom}}
    =
    \left(\frac{1-\sqrt{\gamma_\eps}}
    {1+\sqrt{\gamma_\eps}}\right)^2.
  \end{equation}
  Then the local asymptotic factor of \eqref{eq:momentum-cycle} is
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:optimal-momentum-rate}
    r_{\mathrm{mom}}
    =
    \frac{1-\sqrt{\gamma_\eps}}{1+\sqrt{\gamma_\eps}}.
  \end{equation}
  Thus the convergence gap improves from order $\gamma_\eps$ to order
  $\sqrt{\gamma_\eps}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a mode with $a=1-\lambda\in[\gamma_\eps,1]$, the scalar recurrence is
\[
  z_{\ell+1}
  =
  (1-\omega a+\vartheta)z_\ell-\vartheta z_{\ell-1}.
\]
With \eqref{eq:optimal-momentum-parameters}, put
$r=(1-\sqrt{\gamma_\eps})/(1+\sqrt{\gamma_\eps})$. Then
$\vartheta=r^2$ and $1-\omega a+\vartheta$ ranges from $2r$ to $-2r$
as $a$ ranges from $\gamma_\eps$ to $1$. Both characteristic roots
therefore have modulus at most $r$.
\end{proof}

Over-relaxing the two block updates separately gives the nonlinear SOR
variant studied in \cite{ThibaultEtAl2021,LehmannEtAl2022}. Its optimal
local parameter is
\[
  \omega_{\mathrm{SOR}}
  =
  \frac{2}{1+\sqrt{\gamma_\eps}},
\]
and it has the same factor \eqref{eq:optimal-momentum-rate}. Block SOR
and the outer momentum step \eqref{eq:momentum-cycle} are different
algorithms, despite their identical optimal local rate. Their global
convergence requires safeguards; the displayed parameters are local
spectral prescriptions, not unconditional global guarantees.

\paragraph{Estimating the gap from iterates.}
The optimal parameters depend on an unknown local quantity. During an
ordinary Sinkhorn run, set
\[
  d^{(\ell)}
  :=
  \operatorname{cent}_{\be}\bigl(g^{(\ell)}-g^{(\ell-1)}\bigr).
\]
Near the fixed point,
$d^{(\ell+1)}=T^\ast T d^{(\ell)}+o(\norm{d^{(\ell)}})$, so the Rayleigh
quotient
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:rate-estimator}
  \widehat r_\ell
  :=
  \frac{\ip{d^{(\ell)}}{d^{(\ell+1)}}_\be}
       {\norm{d^{(\ell)}}_\be^2}
  \end{equation}
converges generically to $\sigma_\eps^2$ when a dominant mode is
present. The norm ratio
$\norm{d^{(\ell+1)}}_\be/\norm{d^{(\ell)}}_\be$ is a simpler,
slightly noisier alternative. In practice one clips
$\widehat r_\ell$ to $[0,1)$, sets
$\widehat\gamma_\ell=1-\widehat r_\ell$, and activates acceleration only
after the estimate has stabilized. This also explains why extrapolation
based on early iterates can be unreliable: the asymptotic spectral mode
has not yet emerged.

\section{Semi-dual variable projection}
\label{sec:semidual}

Eliminating one potential exactly converts the same operator
$T^\ast T$ from an iteration Jacobian into a Hessian correction. This
is the variable-projection mechanism: the poorly conditioned block
coupling is removed by a Schur complement rather than handled by
alternating block maximization.

\paragraph{Reduced objective and Hessian.}
For each $g$, the exact source-potential maximizer is
$g^{\bar c,\eps}$ from \eqref{eq:soft-transform-g}. The resulting
concave semi-dual is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:semidual-definition}
  \Ee_\eps(g)
  :=
  \Dd_\eps(g^{\bar c,\eps},g)
  =
  \int_{\Xx}g^{\bar c,\eps}\dd\al
  +
  \int_{\Yy}g\dd\be.
\end{equation}
Let
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:semidual-coupling}
  \dd\pi_g(x,y)
  :=
  \exp\left(
    \frac{g^{\bar c,\eps}(x)+g(y)-c(x,y)}{\eps}
  \right)
  \dd\al(x)\dd\be(y),
\end{equation}
and denote its second marginal by $\be_g$. Its first marginal is
exactly $\al$.

\begin{proposition}[Semi-dual derivatives and Schur complement]
  \label{prop:semidual-varpro}
  For bounded directions $k,k'$ on $\Yy$,
  \begin{align}
    D\Ee_\eps(g)[k]
    &=
    \int_{\Yy} k\,\dd(\be-\be_g),
    \label{eq:semidual-gradient}
    \\
    D^2\Ee_\eps(g)[k,k']
    &=
    -\frac1\eps
    \int_{\Xx}
    \Cov_{\pi_g(\cdot\mid x)}(k(Y),k'(Y))\dd\al(x).
    \label{eq:semidual-hessian-general}
  \end{align}
  At $g=g^\star$, the positive Riesz curvature operator on $L^2(\be)$ is
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:semidual-hessian}
    H_{\mathrm{sd}}
    :=
    -\nabla^2\Ee_\eps(g^\star)
    =
    \frac1\eps(I-T^\ast T).
  \end{equation}
  The identity extends from bounded directions to $L^2(\be)$ by
  density.
  It is exactly the Schur complement of the $f$ block in
  \eqref{eq:full-dual-hessian}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The envelope theorem gives \eqref{eq:semidual-gradient}. Differentiating
the conditional Gibbs law in \eqref{eq:semidual-coupling} gives the
conditional covariance formula \eqref{eq:semidual-hessian-general}.
At the optimum,
\[
  \int_{\Xx}\Cov(k,k'\mid X)\dd\al
  =
  \ip{k}{k'}_\be-\ip{Tk}{Tk'}_\al
  =
  \ip{k}{(I-T^\ast T)k'}_\be,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:semidual-hessian}. Finally, the Schur complement
of $I$ in
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&T\\T^\ast&I\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ is
$I-T^\ast T$. This is exact variable projection in the sense of
Golub and Pereyra \cite{GolubPereyra1973,GolubPereyra2003}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Conditioning and first-order rates.}
The spectral gap that controls Sinkhorn is exactly the strong concavity
of $\Ee_\eps$, or equivalently the strong convexity of $-\Ee_\eps$.

\begin{proposition}[Semi-dual conditioning and optimization rates]
  \label{prop:semidual-conditioning}
  On $L_0^2(\be)$,
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:semidual-curvature}
    \frac{\gamma_\eps}{\eps} I
    \preceq
    H_{\mathrm{sd}}
    \preceq
    \frac1\eps I,
    \qquad
    \kappa_{\mathrm{sd}}
    \leq
    \frac1{\gamma_\eps}.
  \end{equation}
  Gradient ascent on the local quadratic model of $\Ee_\eps$, with
  optimal constant step $2\eps/(1+\gamma_\eps)$, has factor
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:semidual-gradient-rate}
    r_{\mathrm{GD}}
    \leq
    \frac{1-\gamma_\eps}{1+\gamma_\eps}.
  \end{equation}
  Heavy-ball or Chebyshev acceleration has factor
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:semidual-momentum-rate}
    r_{\mathrm{HB}}
    \leq
    \frac{1-\sqrt{\gamma_\eps}}{1+\sqrt{\gamma_\eps}}.
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The spectrum of $T^\ast T$ on centered functions lies in
$[0,1-\gamma_\eps]$, so
the spectrum of \eqref{eq:semidual-hessian} lies in
$[\gamma_\eps/\eps,1/\eps]$. This proves the curvature and condition-number
bounds. The standard minimax polynomials on an interval give
\eqref{eq:semidual-gradient-rate} and
\eqref{eq:semidual-momentum-rate}.
\end{proof}

Since
$\gamma_\eps=(1-\sigma_\eps)(1+\sigma_\eps)$, this condition-number
bound is smaller than the doubly centered full-dual condition number
in \eqref{eq:full-dual-curvature} by the factor
$(1+\sigma_\eps)^2$.

The two rates are not merely analogous to those in
Section~\ref{sec:acceleration}: they are the same formulas. Locally,
\[
  \Ss_\eps(g)-g
  =
  \eps\nabla\Ee_\eps(g)
  +o(\norm{g-g^\star}),
\]
where the gradient is the $L^2(\be)$ Riesz gradient.
Thus optimal scalar extrapolation of Sinkhorn is gradient ascent on the
semi-dual linearization, and \eqref{eq:momentum-cycle} is its heavy-ball
counterpart. Nesterov acceleration gives the same square-root dependence
on $\gamma_\eps$ \cite{Nesterov1983}, although its standard worst-case
constant differs from the
stationary heavy-ball factor \eqref{eq:semidual-momentum-rate}.

\paragraph{Quasi-Newton methods.}
In a finite discretization, $-\Ee_\eps$ is a smooth unconstrained
convex problem after fixing its additive gauge. BFGS attempts to learn
the inverse of $I-T^\ast T$ from gradient differences, precisely the
operator responsible for the slow modes. Under the standard local
smoothness and positive-definiteness assumptions, full BFGS is locally
superlinear; limited-memory BFGS trades that guarantee for lower memory
and often strong practical performance \cite{NocedalWright2006}.

This strategy has a substantial OT history. Cuturi and Peyr\'e used
smooth dual and semi-dual formulations with L-BFGS for variational
Wasserstein problems and developed the general regularized semi-dual
viewpoint \cite{CuturiPeyre2016,CuturiPeyre2018}; stochastic
first-order optimization of semi-duals was developed by Genevay et al.
\cite{GenevayEtAl2016}. Khamlich, Romor, and
Rozza use L-BFGS as the optimization core of a modern
entropy-regularized semi-discrete solver, together with truncation,
multilevel continuation, and fast geometric queries
\cite{KhamlichRomorRozza2026}. Qiu, Yin, and Wang also report and analyze
L-BFGS on the fully discrete entropic dual as an alternative to long
Sinkhorn runs \cite{QiuYinWang2023}.

These methods do not make the small-$\eps$ conditioning disappear: the
inverse Hessian still grows like $1/\gamma_\eps$. Their advantage is that
curvature information is learned and reused. Each quasi-Newton step is
more expensive than one scaling update, so comparisons should count
kernel evaluations or matrix-vector products rather than iterations.
The semi-dual is especially attractive when one block can be eliminated
analytically or integrated accurately, as in semi-discrete transport.

\paragraph{Operator summary.}

All local statements in the note follow from one contraction:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Object & Operator at the optimum & Consequence \\
\midrule
Full dual Hessian
& $\eps^{-1}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&T\\T^\ast&I\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
& coupled conditioning \\
Sinkhorn full-cycle Jacobian
& $T^\ast T$
& local factor $\sigma_\eps^2$ \\
Semi-dual Hessian
& $\eps^{-1}(I-T^\ast T)$
& curvature $\gamma_\eps/\eps$ \\
Accelerated local polynomial
& $I-T^\ast T$
& rate governed by $\sqrt{\gamma_\eps}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Thus the slow convergence of Sinkhorn and the ill-conditioning of the
semi-dual are complementary descriptions of the same near-deterministic
correlation modes. Variable projection removes one coupled block;
extrapolation changes the first-order polynomial; momentum and
quasi-Newton methods approximate the inverse curvature more
aggressively.
