Fractional Generators, and PL/KL Inequalities}
\author{Gabriel Peyr\'e}
\date{July 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
This note asks when the flow of
$\alpha\mapsto\KL(\alpha\mid\beta)$ generated by a reversible jump kernel
satisfies a Polyak--{\L}ojasiewicz (PL), or more generally a
Kurdyka--{\L}ojasiewicz (KL), inequality.  At the semigroup level, the exact
condition is the \emph{modified logarithmic Sobolev inequality} (MLSI).  It
compares relative entropy with the nonlocal Fisher information
$\cE_K(r,\log r)$, where $r=\dd\alpha/\dd\beta$.  It becomes a genuine metric
PL inequality once the logarithmic-mean transport metric has this Fisher
information as the squared entropy slope.  This distinction is automatic in
standard finite-state and translation-invariant frameworks, but requires
care for singular target-dependent kernels.  In every case the answer is not
a property of $\beta$ alone: it depends essentially on the jump kernel.

Several useful sufficient conditions are established: positive geodesic
curvature of the entropy, a Gross or weak log-Sobolev inequality for the jump
Dirichlet form, a Doeblin minorization, and a spectral gap on classes of
uniformly bounded densities.  An exact two-level test quantifies bottleneck
obstructions.  A particularly clean positive construction is obtained by
subordinating the reversible Langevin semigroup.  If $\beta$
satisfies the ordinary log-Sobolev inequality with constant $\lambda$ and
$\phi$ is a Bernstein function, then the nonlocal generator
$-\phi(-L_\beta)$ satisfies MLSI with constant at least
$\phi(2\lambda)/2$.  For the standard Gaussian and
$\phi(q)=q^{s/2}$, this gives a Gaussian-reversible fractional
Ornstein--Uhlenbeck flow with MLSI constant at least $2^{s/2-1}$.
This is not the same as adding a translation-invariant fractional Laplacian
to an Ornstein--Uhlenbeck drift, whose invariant law is stable rather than
Gaussian when $s<2$ and which is generally not reversible.  Direct
stable-like reversible forms provide a second positive construction for the
stretched-exponential family $e^{-(1+\abs x^2)^q}$, including log-concave
targets with tails heavier than Gaussian that fail the ordinary local Gross
LSI.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and main conclusions}

Let $\beta$ be a probability measure and consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropy-energy}
  f(\alpha)=\KL(\alpha\mid\beta).
\end{equation}
For the ordinary $2$-Wasserstein geometry, the squared metric slope of
\eqref{eq:entropy-energy} is the relative Fisher information.  The classical
logarithmic Sobolev inequality is therefore exactly the Wasserstein--PL
inequality for $f$.  The same dictionary survives for the nonlocal
Wasserstein geometry generated by a reversible jump kernel, but the Fisher
information becomes a pair-space quantity.

The conclusions of this note are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=2.2em]
  \item For a fixed reversible kernel $K$, the exact semigroup condition is
  the modified logarithmic Sobolev inequality (MLSI)
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:executive-mlsi}
    \cI_K(r)\geq 2\kappa\Ent_\beta(r),
    \qquad r=\frac{\dd\alpha}{\dd\beta}.
  \end{equation}
  It is equivalent to uniform exponential entropy decay of the jump
  semigroup.  It is also the metric PL inequality whenever the nonlocal
  transport metric satisfies the entropy-slope identity.
  \item No assumption on $\beta$ alone can imply
  \eqref{eq:executive-mlsi}.  The kernel can be slowed down, disconnected, or
  made arbitrarily weak in the tails without changing $\beta$.
  \item Mere log-concavity of $\beta$ does not settle the question.  Strong
  log-concavity does give a positive result for a canonical family of
  nonlocal kernels: subordinate the reversible Langevin generator.
  \item The translation-invariant fractional Laplacian on $\R^d$ is
  reversible with respect to Lebesgue measure, not a confining probability
  measure.  It therefore does not directly define the gradient flow of
  $\KL(\cdot\mid\beta)$ for a Gaussian or another nonconstant target.
  \item For $\beta=\mathcal N(0,I_d)$, the fractional power of the
  Ornstein--Uhlenbeck generator is a genuine Gaussian-reversible nonlocal
  model.  Its jump kernel is explicit as a mixture of Mehler kernels, and it
  satisfies a quantitative MLSI.
  \item A direct stable-like reversible kernel also satisfies MLSI for
  $\beta_q\propto e^{-(1+\abs x^2)^q}$ for every $q>0$.  The range
  $1/2\leq q<1$ is especially informative: these targets are log-concave but
  fail the ordinary local Gross LSI, while the long-jump form remains
  entropy-coercive.
\end{enumerate}

The word ``KL'' is ambiguous in this discussion.  We write $\KL$ for the
Kullback--Leibler divergence and reserve ``power-KL inequality'' for a
Kurdyka--{\L}ojasiewicz slope inequality.

\section{Reversible jump geometry and entropy dissipation}

\subsection{Kernel, edge measure, and Dirichlet form}

Let $(\mathcal X,\beta)$ be a probability space.  A jump kernel
$K(x,\dd y)$ is \emph{reversible} with respect to $\beta$ when the edge
measure
\begin{equation}\label{eq:edge-measure}
  \Jm_\beta(\dd x,\dd y)
  \coloneqq \beta(\dd x)K(x,\dd y)
\end{equation}
is symmetric under $(x,y)\mapsto(y,x)$.  The generator and its Dirichlet form
are
\begin{align}
  L_K h(x)
  &\coloneqq \int_{\mathcal X}\bigl(h(y)-h(x)\bigr)K(x,\dd y),
  \label{eq:jump-generator}\\
  \cE_K(h,g)
  &\coloneqq -\int hL_Kg\,\dd\beta
   =\frac12\iint
       \bgrad h(x,y)\bgrad g(x,y)\,
       \Jm_\beta(\dd x,\dd y),
  \label{eq:jump-dirichlet}
\end{align}
where $\bgrad h(x,y)=h(y)-h(x)$.  Singular kernels are interpreted through
the closed Dirichlet form or through principal values.

For $\alpha=r\beta$, with $r\geq0$ and $\int r\,\dd\beta=1$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-entropy-density}
  \cH_\beta(r)
  \coloneqq \KL(\alpha\mid\beta)
  =\int r\log r\,\dd\beta.
\end{equation}
The nonlocal Fisher information associated with $K$ is
\begin{align}
  \cI_K(r)
  &\coloneqq \cE_K(r,\log r)\label{eq:nonlocal-fisher}\\
  &=\frac12\iint
      \bigl(r(y)-r(x)\bigr)
      \bigl(\log r(y)-\log r(x)\bigr)
      \,\Jm_\beta(\dd x,\dd y).
  \notag
\end{align}
The integrand is nonnegative.  Equivalently, using the logarithmic mean
\[
  \theta(a,b)=\frac{a-b}{\log a-\log b},
\]
one can write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonlocal-fisher-logmean}
  \cI_K(r)
  =\frac12\iint
  \abs{\bgrad\log r(x,y)}^2
  \theta(r(x),r(y))\,
  \Jm_\beta(\dd x,\dd y).
\end{equation}
The logarithmic mean is what converts the entropy gradient into the linear
jump evolution.  To make the metric statement precise, however, one first
needs the underlying dynamic action.

\subsection{Nonlocal action and path metric}

Let $r$ be a density with respect to $\beta$ and let $j(x,y)=-j(y,x)$ be an
antisymmetric flux density with respect to $\Jm_\beta$.  Its logarithmic-mean
action is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonlocal-action}
  \mathcal A_K(r,j)
  \coloneqq \frac12\iint
  \frac{j(x,y)^2}{\theta(r(x),r(y))}\,
  \Jm_\beta(\dd x,\dd y),
\end{equation}
with the usual lower-semicontinuous convention at zero.  A curve
$t\mapsto r_t$ and a flux $j_t$ solve the nonlocal continuity equation when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonlocal-continuity}
  \frac{\dd}{\dd t}\int \varphi r_t\,\dd\beta
  =\frac12\iint \bgrad\varphi(x,y)j_t(x,y)
    \,\Jm_\beta(\dd x,\dd y)
\end{equation}
for every admissible test function $\varphi$.  The associated extended path
distance is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonlocal-distance}
  \cW_K^2(r_0\beta,r_1\beta)
  \coloneqq \inf_{(r_t,j_t)}
  \int_0^1\mathcal A_K(r_t,j_t)\,\dd t,
\end{equation}
where the infimum is over solutions of \eqref{eq:nonlocal-continuity} with
the prescribed endpoints.  For singular kernels, the rigorous relaxation
allows measure-valued fluxes rather than only densities $j_t$.

This construction is the jump analogue of Benamou--Brenier transport.  Its
metric and geodesic properties have been established under broad hypotheses
by \citet{Erbar2014JumpEntropy,SlepcevWarren2023}; the distance may be
infinite between distinct finite-action components.

\subsection{Semigroup dissipation versus metric slope}

The reversible semigroup $P_t=e^{tL_K}$ evolves densities according to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jump-flow}
  \partial_t r_t=L_Kr_t,
  \qquad \alpha_t=r_t\beta.
\end{equation}
Whenever differentiation is justified, reversibility alone gives the
entropy-dissipation identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropy-dissipation}
  -\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\cH_\beta(r_t)=\cI_K(r_t).
\end{equation}
Indeed, differentiating \eqref{eq:relative-entropy-density} yields
$\int(1+\log r_t)L_Kr_t\,\dd\beta=-\cE_K(r_t,\log r_t)$.

The stronger metric statement uses the flux
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropy-flux}
  j_t(x,y)=-\theta(r_t(x),r_t(y))\bgrad\log r_t(x,y)
  =r_t(x)-r_t(y),
\end{equation}
which turns \eqref{eq:nonlocal-continuity} into \eqref{eq:jump-flow} and
turns \eqref{eq:nonlocal-action} into $\cI_K(r_t)$.  In the settings where
the corresponding upper-gradient theory is valid, one obtains
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slope-identity}
  \abs{\partial\cH_\beta}_{\cW_K}^2(r)=\cI_K(r)
\end{equation}
and $P_t r$ is the $\cW_K$-gradient flow of entropy.  This is rigorous for
the translation-invariant framework of \citet{Erbar2014JumpEntropy} and for
the standard finite-state theory \citep{ErbarMaas2012}; extending the identification to a given
singular, target-dependent kernel requires checking the metric-domain and
lower-semicontinuity hypotheses.  Below, semigroup statements use only
\eqref{eq:entropy-dissipation}; every claim phrased as a metric PL statement
additionally assumes \eqref{eq:slope-identity}.

\section{MLSI, entropy decay, and metric PL}

\subsection{MLSI and exponential entropy decay}

\begin{definition}[Modified log-Sobolev inequality]
\label{def:nonlocal-pl}
The pair $(\beta,K)$ satisfies the modified log-Sobolev inequality with constant
$\kappa>0$ if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonlocal-pl}
  \cI_K(r)
  \geq 2\kappa\cH_\beta(r)
\end{equation}
for every probability density $r$ in the form domain.  The optimal constant is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:optimal-mlsi-constant}
  \kappa_{\rm MLSI}(\beta,K)
  =\frac12\inf_{\substack{r\geq0,\ \int r\dd\beta=1\\
                          \cH_\beta(r)>0}}
    \frac{\cI_K(r)}{\cH_\beta(r)}.
\end{equation}
When \eqref{eq:slope-identity} holds, this is also the optimal metric PL
constant of $\cH_\beta$ for $\cW_K$.
\end{definition}

The adjective ``modified'' matters.  For local diffusions, the chain rule
identifies the entropy production with four times the Dirichlet energy of
$\sqrt r$.  For jump generators there is no exact chain rule, and
$\cE_K(r,\log r)$ is not the same object as
$4\cE_K(\sqrt r,\sqrt r)$.  Modified log-Sobolev inequalities are therefore
the natural entropy-production inequalities for jump processes; see
\citet{BobkovTetali2006,AdamczakPolaczykStrzelecki2022}.

\begin{theorem}[MLSI is equivalent to exponential entropy decay]
\label{thm:mlsi-equivalence}
Assume that the reversible semigroup $P_t=e^{tL_K}$ satisfies the entropy
dissipation identity \eqref{eq:entropy-dissipation}.  Then the following are
equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item The modified log-Sobolev inequality \eqref{eq:nonlocal-pl} holds with constant
  $\kappa$.
  \item For every probability density $r$ and every $t\geq0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:entropy-contraction}
    \cH_\beta(P_t r)
    \leq e^{-2\kappa t}\cH_\beta(r).
  \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
If, in addition, \eqref{eq:slope-identity} holds, $P_t r$ is the
$\cW_K$-gradient flow, and $P_t r\to1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonlocal-talagrand}
  \cW_K^2(\alpha,\beta)
  \leq \frac{2}{\kappa}\KL(\alpha\mid\beta)
\end{equation}
for every $\alpha$ in the same finite-distance component as $\beta$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Along the semigroup, \eqref{eq:entropy-dissipation} and
\eqref{eq:nonlocal-pl} give
\[
  \frac{\dd}{\dd t}\cH_\beta(P_t r)
  \leq -2\kappa\cH_\beta(P_t r),
\]
and Gronwall's lemma proves \eqref{eq:entropy-contraction}.  Conversely,
differentiate \eqref{eq:entropy-contraction} at $t=0^+$.  The dissipation
identity $-\frac{\dd}{\dd t}|_{t=0^+}\cH_\beta(P_t r)=\cI_K(r)$ gives the
reverse implication.

For the last assertion, the standard PL tail-length argument in the metric
space $(\Pp(\mathcal X),\cW_K)$ gives
\[
  \cW_K(\alpha,\beta)
  \leq \int_0^\infty\abs{\dot\alpha_t}_{\cW_K}\,\dd t
  \leq \sqrt{\frac{2}{\kappa}}\sqrt{\cH_\beta(r)}.
\]
Squaring yields \eqref{eq:nonlocal-talagrand}.
\end{proof}

Thus the requested ``fractional log-Sobolev inequality'' is exactly MLSI at
the semigroup level and, under \eqref{eq:slope-identity}, exactly PL in the
reversible nonlocal Wasserstein geometry.  What changes with the kernel is
the entropy production $\cI_K$.

\subsection{Linearization: a spectral gap is necessary}

Let $h$ be bounded, centered under $\beta$, and set
$r_\varepsilon=1+\varepsilon h$.  As $\varepsilon\to0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linearized-entropy-fisher}
  \cH_\beta(r_\varepsilon)
  =\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2}\int h^2\,\dd\beta+O(\varepsilon^3),
  \qquad
  \cI_K(r_\varepsilon)
  =\varepsilon^2\cE_K(h,h)+O(\varepsilon^3).
\end{equation}
Consequently:

\begin{proposition}[PL implies the jump Poincar\'e inequality]
\label{prop:pl-implies-gap}
If \eqref{eq:nonlocal-pl} holds with constant $\kappa$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jump-poincare}
  \cE_K(h,h)\geq\kappa\Var_\beta(h)
\end{equation}
for every $h$ in the Dirichlet-form domain.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Insert \eqref{eq:linearized-entropy-fisher} into
\eqref{eq:nonlocal-pl}, divide by $\varepsilon^2$, and let
$\varepsilon\to0$.
\end{proof}

The converse is false without additional assumptions: a spectral gap is an
$L^2$ statement near equilibrium, whereas MLSI controls arbitrary densities,
including highly concentrated ones.

\subsection{Kurdyka--{\L}ojasiewicz profiles}

The power law is only one possible weakening of PL.  Let
$\omega:[0,M)\to[0,\infty)$ be continuous at zero, increasing and concave,
with $\omega(0)=0$ and $\omega\in C^1((0,M))$.  A nonlocal KL inequality on
the entropy sublevel $\{0<\cH_\beta<M\}$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-nonlocal-kl}
  \omega'(\cH_\beta(r))\sqrt{\cI_K(r)}\geq1.
\end{equation}
Along the semigroup, this is an entropy--entropy-production inequality.  If
the metric slope identity \eqref{eq:slope-identity} also holds, it is the
usual metric Kurdyka--{\L}ojasiewicz inequality and bounds the remaining
metric length by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kl-tail-length}
  \int_t^\infty\abs{\dot\alpha_s}_{\cW_K}\,\dd s
  \leq \omega(\cH_\beta(r_t)).
\end{equation}
Indeed, combine $-\dot{\cH}_\beta=\cI_K$ with
$\abs{\dot\alpha_t}_{\cW_K}=\sqrt{\cI_K(r_t)}$ and integrate
\eqref{eq:general-nonlocal-kl}.

The most transparent family is the power profile.  Taking
$\omega(s)=s^{1-\vartheta}/[c(1-\vartheta)]$ in
\eqref{eq:general-nonlocal-kl} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonlocal-power-kl}
  \sqrt{\cI_K(r)}
  \geq c\,\cH_\beta(r)^\vartheta,
  \qquad \vartheta\in[0,1),
\end{equation}
on a specified entropy sublevel set or regularity class.  The case
$\vartheta=1/2$ is MLSI, and also metric PL when
\eqref{eq:slope-identity} holds, with $c=\sqrt{2\kappa}$.

\begin{proposition}[Entropy rates under nonlocal power-KL]
\label{prop:power-kl-rates}
Assume \eqref{eq:nonlocal-power-kl} along the entropy flow.  If
$\vartheta>1/2$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power-kl-rate}
  \cH_\beta(r_t)
  \leq
  \left(
  \cH_\beta(r_0)^{1-2\vartheta}
  +c^2(2\vartheta-1)t
  \right)^{-1/(2\vartheta-1)}.
\end{equation}
If $\vartheta=1/2$, the decay is exponential with rate $c^2$.
Moreover, a global inequality with $\vartheta<1/2$ is impossible near a
nondegenerate equilibrium whenever the tangent Dirichlet form is nonzero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The dissipation identity gives
$\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\cH_\beta(r_t)=-\cI_K(r_t)
\leq-c^2\cH_\beta(r_t)^{2\vartheta}$, whose integration yields
\eqref{eq:power-kl-rate}.  For the last assertion, insert
$r_\varepsilon=1+\varepsilon h$ into
\eqref{eq:nonlocal-power-kl}.  By
\eqref{eq:linearized-entropy-fisher}, its two sides scale respectively as
$\abs\varepsilon$ and $\abs\varepsilon^{2\vartheta}$.  If
$\vartheta<1/2$, the right-hand side dominates as
$\varepsilon\to0$, which is impossible for a fixed $c>0$.
\end{proof}

Power-KL inequalities are useful for obtaining polynomial rates, but they do
not remove the need to specify the kernel.  They are entropy--entropy
production inequalities for the pair $(\beta,K)$ just as MLSI is.

\section{Why the target measure alone cannot decide PL}

\subsection{Scaling, connectivity, and bottlenecks}

Replacing $K$ by $\delta K$ multiplies $L_K$, $\cE_K$, and $\cI_K$ by
$\delta$, and divides $\cW_K$ by $\sqrt\delta$.  Thus the optimal MLSI
constant scales as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel-scaling-pl}
  \kappa(\beta,\delta K)=\delta\kappa(\beta,K).
\end{equation}
No quantitative MLSI constant can therefore follow from $\beta$ alone.
If $K$ does not connect two positive-$\beta$ sets, a density that is constant
on each component has positive entropy and zero dissipation, so even the
existence of a positive constant fails.

The obstruction can be quantified exactly by testing two-level densities.
For a measurable set $A$, write $p=\beta(A)$ and define its edge conductance
\begin{equation}\label{eq:edge-conductance}
  Q_K(A,A^c)\coloneqq\Jm_\beta(A\times A^c).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact two-level bottleneck test]
\label{prop:two-level-bottleneck}
Let $0<p<1$, $q\in(0,1)$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-level-density}
  r_{A,q}=\frac{q}{p}\one_A+
          \frac{1-q}{1-p}\one_{A^c}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
  \cH_\beta(r_{A,q})
  &=q\log\frac qp+(1-q)\log\frac{1-q}{1-p},
  \label{eq:two-level-entropy}\\
  \cI_K(r_{A,q})
  &=Q_K(A,A^c)
    \left(\frac qp-\frac{1-q}{1-p}\right)
    \log\frac{q(1-p)}{p(1-q)}.
  \label{eq:two-level-information}
\end{align}
Consequently \eqref{eq:optimal-mlsi-constant} is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bottleneck-mlsi-upper}
  \frac{Q_K(A,A^c)}{2\cH_\beta(r_{A,q})}
    \left(\frac qp-\frac{1-q}{1-p}\right)
    \log\frac{q(1-p)}{p(1-q)}.
\end{equation}
As $q\to p$, this upper bound tends to
$Q_K(A,A^c)/[p(1-p)]$, the Rayleigh quotient of $\one_A$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The density \eqref{eq:two-level-density} assigns masses $q$ and $1-q$ to
$A$ and $A^c$, so direct integration gives
\eqref{eq:two-level-entropy}.  In \eqref{eq:nonlocal-fisher}, pairs lying in
the same set contribute zero.  Symmetry of $\Jm_\beta$ cancels the factor
$1/2$ between the two orientations of $A\times A^c$ and gives
\eqref{eq:two-level-information}.  Inserting this test density into
\eqref{eq:optimal-mlsi-constant} proves \eqref{eq:bottleneck-mlsi-upper}.
Finally, writing $q=p+\delta$ gives
\begin{align*}
  \cH_\beta(r_{A,p+\delta})
  &=\frac{\delta^2}{2p(1-p)}+o(\delta^2),\\
  \cI_K(r_{A,p+\delta})
  &=\frac{Q_K(A,A^c)\delta^2}{p^2(1-p)^2}+o(\delta^2),
\end{align*}
which proves the limit.
\end{proof}

This proposition separates two obstructions.  Taking $q\to p$ detects the
linearized spectral gap, while taking $q$ far from $p$ probes the genuinely
nonlinear entropy cost of concentrating mass behind a bottleneck.
Irreducibility by itself does not rule either out.  The following example
fixes the target to be Gaussian and uses an everywhere-positive kernel.

\begin{proposition}[A Gaussian target with no MLSI]
\label{prop:gaussian-bad-kernel}
Let $\beta=\mathcal N(0,I_d)$ and let $a:\R^d\to(0,1]$ satisfy
$a(x)\to0$ as $\abs x\to\infty$, for instance
$a(x)=e^{-\abs x^2}$.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bad-gaussian-kernel}
  K_a(x,\dd y)=a(x)a(y)\beta(\dd y).
\end{equation}
Then $K_a$ is everywhere positive and reversible with respect to $\beta$,
but its Dirichlet form has no positive spectral gap.  Consequently the pair
has no positive MLSI constant and, whenever \eqref{eq:slope-identity} holds,
$\KL(\cdot\mid\beta)$ satisfies no global metric PL inequality.  For the
specific choice $a(x)=e^{-\abs x^2}$, it satisfies no power-KL inequality
\eqref{eq:nonlocal-power-kl} with any $\vartheta\in[0,1)$ on any nontrivial
entropy sublevel $\{r:\cH_\beta(r)\leq M\}$, $M>0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The edge measure
$a(x)a(y)\beta(\dd x)\beta(\dd y)$ is symmetric, hence the kernel is
reversible.  Let $A_R=\{x:\abs x\geq R\}$ and $p_R=\beta(A_R)$.  Its
conductance factorizes as
\[
  Q_{K_a}(A_R,A_R^c)
  =\left(\int_{A_R}a\,\dd\beta\right)
   \left(\int_{A_R^c}a\,\dd\beta\right)
  \leq p_R\sup_{A_R}a.
\]
The $q\to p_R$ limit in Proposition~\ref{prop:two-level-bottleneck} is at
most $\sup_{A_R}a/(1-p_R)$ and tends to zero.  Hence the spectral gap and
MLSI constants both vanish.

For the stronger assertion, fix $h>0$, set
$L_R=\log(1/p_R)$ and $q_R=h/L_R$, and consider the two-level density
\[
  r_R=r_{A_R,q_R}.
\]
As $R\to\infty$, direct substitution gives
$\cH_\beta(r_R)\to h$.  On the other hand, the factorization of the edge
measure in \eqref{eq:bad-gaussian-kernel} gives
\begin{align*}
  \cI_{K_a}(r_R)
  ={}&Q_{K_a}(A_R,A_R^c)
      \left(\frac{q_R}{p_R}-\frac{1-q_R}{1-p_R}\right)\\
     &\times
      \log\!\left(\frac{q_R(1-p_R)}{p_R(1-q_R)}\right).
\end{align*}
For $a(x)=e^{-\abs x^2}$, this is
$O(e^{-R^2}q_RL_R)=O(e^{-R^2})$, hence tends to zero.  Thus a positive
power-KL constant is impossible on $\{r:\cH_\beta(r)\leq M\}$: choose any
$h\in(0,M)$ and take $R$ sufficiently large.
\end{proof}

This example is the clean reason that ``$\beta$ is Gaussian'' or
``$\beta$ is strongly log-concave'' cannot by itself answer the question.
One must specify how the process is allowed to jump.

\section{Verifiable sufficient conditions}

\subsection{Positive entropic curvature}

The geometric sufficient condition is the exact analogue of the local
Wasserstein argument.

\begin{proposition}[Positive nonlocal entropy curvature implies PL]
\label{prop:curvature-implies-nonlocal-pl}
Assume that $\cH_\beta$ is $\lambda$-geodesically convex in
$(\Pp(\mathcal X),\cW_K)$ for some $\lambda>0$, and that its unique minimizer
is $\beta$.  Then \eqref{eq:nonlocal-pl} holds with
$\kappa=\lambda$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The standard metric slope estimate for a $\lambda$-geodesically convex
functional gives
$\abs{\partial\cH_\beta}_{\cW_K}^2
\geq2\lambda\cH_\beta$.  Use the slope identity
\eqref{eq:slope-identity}.
\end{proof}

This is the entropic Ricci-curvature route developed for Markov chains by
\citet{ErbarMaas2012}.  For the translation-invariant L\'evy kernels treated
by \citet{Erbar2014JumpEntropy}, the proved statement is only
$0$-geodesic convexity.  It gives contractivity but no positive PL constant;
strict curvature for concrete singular nonhomogeneous kernels is much harder
to verify.

\subsection{Gross log-Sobolev implies modified log-Sobolev}

Suppose the jump Dirichlet form satisfies the Gross inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gross-jump-lsi}
  \Ent_\beta(h^2)
  \leq \frac{2}{\lambda_G}\cE_K(h,h).
\end{equation}
This is not the same inequality as \eqref{eq:nonlocal-pl}, but it is a useful
strong sufficient condition.

\begin{proposition}[Gross LSI implies MLSI]
\label{prop:gross-implies-modified}
If \eqref{eq:gross-jump-lsi} holds, then
$\cI_K(r)\geq2\lambda_G\cH_\beta(r)$.  Thus the MLSI constant is at least
$\lambda_G$; under \eqref{eq:slope-identity}, so is the metric PL constant.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $a,b>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jump-chain-rule-inequality}
  (a-b)(\log a-\log b)
  \geq4(\sqrt a-\sqrt b)^2.
\end{equation}
Integrating this inequality against $\Jm_\beta/2$ gives
$\cI_K(r)\geq4\cE_K(\sqrt r,\sqrt r)$.  Apply
\eqref{eq:gross-jump-lsi} to $h=\sqrt r$, for which
$\Ent_\beta(h^2)=\cH_\beta(r)$.
\end{proof}

The inequality \eqref{eq:jump-chain-rule-inequality} replaces the exact
diffusion chain rule.  It is also the bridge allowing the substantial
literature on Poincar\'e, super-Poincar\'e, and Gross log-Sobolev inequalities
for stable-like Dirichlet forms to provide PL results for the nonlocal
Wasserstein entropy flow.

\subsection{A Doeblin or refresh minorization}

The simplest nonlocal kernel is the refresh kernel
$K_{\rm ref}(x,\dd y)=\beta(\dd y)$.  It forgets the current state and draws
a new state from equilibrium.  Its entropy production is explicit:
\begin{align}
  \cI_{\rm ref}(r)
  &=\frac12\iint(r(x)-r(y))(\log r(x)-\log r(y))
       \,\dd\beta(x)\dd\beta(y)\notag\\
  &=\cH_\beta(r)-\int\log r\,\dd\beta
  =\KL(\alpha\mid\beta)+\KL(\beta\mid\alpha)
  \geq\cH_\beta(r).
  \label{eq:refresh-information}
\end{align}

\begin{proposition}[Doeblin minorization implies MLSI]
\label{prop:doeblin-pl}
Assume the symmetric edge measure satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:doeblin-edge-minorization}
  \Jm_\beta(\dd x,\dd y)
  \geq c\,\beta(\dd x)\beta(\dd y)
\end{equation}
for some $c>0$.  Then
$\cI_K(r)\geq c\cH_\beta(r)$, so
\eqref{eq:nonlocal-pl} holds with $\kappa=c/2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The integrand in \eqref{eq:nonlocal-fisher} is nonnegative.  Compare the
edge measure with \eqref{eq:refresh-information} and use Jensen's inequality
$\int\log r\,\dd\beta\leq\log\int r\,\dd\beta=0$.
\end{proof}

This condition is strong, but it makes the roles of the target and the kernel
transparent: $\beta$ supplies the refresh law, while $c$ quantifies the jump
mechanism's access to it.

\subsection{Spectral gap plus bounded densities}

A spectral gap becomes sufficient after restricting the density ratio.

\begin{proposition}[MLSI on an $L^\infty$ density class]
\label{prop:bounded-density-pl}
Assume the Poincar\'e inequality
$\cE_K(h,h)\geq\lambda_P\Var_\beta(h)$ and let
$r$ be a probability density satisfying $0\leq r\leq M$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-density-mlsi}
  \cI_K(r)
  \geq \frac{\lambda_P}{M}\cH_\beta(r).
\end{equation}
Thus MLSI holds on this class with constant
$\kappa=\lambda_P/(2M)$; it is a restricted metric PL estimate under
\eqref{eq:slope-identity}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $0<a,b\leq M$, the mean-value theorem gives
$(a-b)(\log a-\log b)\geq(a-b)^2/M$.  Hence
$\cI_K(r)\geq\cE_K(r,r)/M$.  Moreover,
$\cH_\beta(r)\leq\int(r-1)^2\,\dd\beta$ because
$u\log u-u+1\leq(u-1)^2$.  The Poincar\'e inequality concludes the proof.
\end{proof}

This estimate can yield \emph{eventual} exponential entropy decay when the
jump semigroup regularizes arbitrary initial data into a bounded density
class.  The constant then depends on the regularization bound and is not a
global MLSI constant uniform over all initial densities.

\subsection{Weak log-Sobolev inequalities give KL profiles}

When a global MLSI fails, a weak log-Sobolev inequality can still quantify
the decay.  Assume that a decreasing profile $B_{\rm WL}:(0,\infty)\to
(0,\infty)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weak-gross-lsi}
  \Ent_\beta(h^2)
  \leq B_{\rm WL}(s)\cE_K(h,h)
       +s\operatorname{Osc}(h)^2,
  \qquad s>0,
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{Osc}(h)=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}h-
\operatorname*{ess\,inf}h$.  Such inequalities and their relation to entropy
decay are developed by \citet{CattiauxGentilGuillin2007}.

\begin{proposition}[Weak LSI yields a restricted nonlocal KL inequality]
\label{prop:weak-lsi-kl-profile}
Assume \eqref{eq:weak-gross-lsi}.  Let $r_t=P_t r_0$, with $r_0$ bounded, and
set $\Omega=\operatorname{Osc}(\sqrt{r_0})$.  Whenever
$\cH_\beta(r_t)>0$ and $\Omega>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weak-lsi-information-profile}
  \cI_K(r_t)
  \geq
  \frac{2\cH_\beta(r_t)}
       {B_{\rm WL}\!\left(\cH_\beta(r_t)/(2\Omega^2)\right)}.
\end{equation}
In particular, if $B_{\rm WL}(s)\leq Cs^{-a}$ for some $a>0$, then
\begin{align}
  \cI_K(r_t)
  &\geq \frac{2^{1-a}}{C\Omega^{2a}}
      \cH_\beta(r_t)^{1+a},
  \label{eq:weak-lsi-power-kl}\\
  \cH_\beta(r_t)
  &\leq
  \left[
    \cH_\beta(r_0)^{-a}
    +\frac{a2^{1-a}}{C\Omega^{2a}}t
  \right]^{-1/a}.
  \label{eq:weak-lsi-entropy-rate}
\end{align}
If $0<a<1$, this is a genuine power-KL inequality with exponent
$\vartheta=(1+a)/2\in(1/2,1)$ on the class with fixed oscillation bound.
Under \eqref{eq:slope-identity}, it controls the metric tail length through
\eqref{eq:kl-tail-length}.  For $a\geq1$, the same entropy-production bound
and polynomial rate remain valid, but the formal power exponent is at least
one and no finite desingularizer $\omega$ at zero follows from this argument.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply \eqref{eq:weak-gross-lsi} to $h=\sqrt{r_t}$ and use
\eqref{eq:jump-chain-rule-inequality} to obtain
\[
  \cH_\beta(r_t)
  \leq \frac14B_{\rm WL}(s)\cI_K(r_t)
      +s\operatorname{Osc}(\sqrt{r_t})^2.
\]
The Markov property keeps $P_t r_0$ between the essential infimum and
supremum of $r_0$, hence
$\operatorname{Osc}(\sqrt{r_t})\leq\Omega$.  Choosing
$s=\cH_\beta(r_t)/(2\Omega^2)$ and rearranging proves
\eqref{eq:weak-lsi-information-profile}.  The polynomial bound on
$B_{\rm WL}$ gives \eqref{eq:weak-lsi-power-kl}; integrating
$-\dot{\cH}_\beta=\cI_K$ then yields
\eqref{eq:weak-lsi-entropy-rate}.
\end{proof}

The dependence on $\Omega$ is essential: weak functional inequalities do not
produce a uniform global KL constant over arbitrarily concentrated initial
densities.  They instead give a hierarchy of rates on controlled classes,
which is precisely the regime missed by a binary PL/no-PL diagnosis.

\subsection{Comparison of kernels}

If two reversible edge measures satisfy
$\Jm_1\geq c\Jm_0$, then
$\cI_{K_1}(r)\geq c\cI_{K_0}(r)$.  Consequently any PL, power-KL, Gross
log-Sobolev, or Poincar\'e estimate for $K_0$ transfers to $K_1$ with the
corresponding factor.  This comparison principle is often more practical
than computing nonlocal curvature.

\section{Why the pure fractional Laplacian is not a confined entropy flow}

\subsection{Translation invariance fixes the reversible measure}

Consider a symmetric translation-invariant jump kernel
\begin{equation}\label{eq:translation-invariant-kernel}
  K(x,\dd y)=q(y-x)\,\dd y,
  \qquad q(z)=q(-z).
\end{equation}
The fractional Laplacian corresponds to
$q(z)=c_{d,s}\abs z^{-d-s}$, $0<s<2$.

\begin{proposition}[No nonconstant reversible target]
\label{prop:translation-invariant-reversibility}
Let $\beta(\dd x)=p(x)\dd x$ with $p>0$ a.e.  Assume that the support of
$q$ generates $\R^d$ as an additive group.  If
\eqref{eq:translation-invariant-kernel} is reversible with respect to
$\beta$, then $p$ is constant a.e.  In particular, on $\R^d$ no probability
measure, Gaussian or otherwise, is reversible for the pure fractional
Laplacian.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Detailed balance reads
$p(x)q(y-x)=p(y)q(x-y)$.  By symmetry of $q$, this gives $p(x)=p(y)$
whenever $q(y-x)>0$.  Connectivity under sums of admissible increments makes
$p$ constant.
\end{proof}

For the pure fractional heat equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pure-fractional-heat}
  \partial_t\rho_t=-(-\Delta)^{s/2}\rho_t,
\end{equation}
the reference measure is Lebesgue measure.  There is no invariant probability
and no finite entropy minimizer.  Indeed, for
$\rho_R(x)=R^{-d}\rho(x/R)$,
\[
  \int\rho_R\log\rho_R\,\dd x
  =\int\rho\log\rho\,\dd x-d\log R\longrightarrow-\infty.
\]
Therefore a global PL inequality around a probability equilibrium is not the
right question for \eqref{eq:pure-fractional-heat}.  Euclidean fractional
log-Sobolev inequalities do exist, but they are scale-sensitive Sobolev
inequalities used for smoothing and ultracontractivity, not MLSI around a
confining target; see \citet{GentilImbert2009,Gressman2014FractionalLSI}.

\subsection{Adding a drift changes the invariant law and may break reversibility}

The fractional Fokker--Planck equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stable-langevin}
  \partial_t\rho_t
  =\nabla\cdot(\rho_t\nabla V)-(-\Delta)^{s/2}\rho_t
\end{equation}
is the forward equation of a Langevin SDE driven by symmetric stable noise.
For $s<2$, its invariant law is generally \emph{not}
$Z^{-1}e^{-V}\dd x$.  Even for the quadratic drift $\nabla V(x)=x$, the
invariant law is $s$-stable rather than Gaussian.  Moreover this L\'evy-driven
Ornstein--Uhlenbeck process is generally not reversible when $s<2$, so its
entropy production need not be the metric slope for the symmetric nonlocal
Wasserstein distance considered here.

\citet{GentilImbert2009} prove modified $\Phi$-entropy inequalities and
exponential convergence for such L\'evy--Fokker--Planck equations.  In the
$s$-stable Ornstein--Uhlenbeck case their entropy estimate has the form
$\Ent(r_t)\leq e^{-st}\Ent(r_0)$.  This is an important entropy-production
result, but for $s<2$ it should not be silently identified with a PL theorem
for a reversible nonlocal Wasserstein metric.  The reversible Gaussian model
constructed below is a different operator.

\section{A canonical positive construction by subordination}

\subsection{Start from a reversible Langevin semigroup}

Let
\[
  \beta(\dd x)=Z^{-1}e^{-V(x)}\dd x
\]
and consider the reversible Langevin generator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:langevin-generator}
  L_\beta h=\Delta h-\nabla V\cdot\nabla h.
\end{equation}
Write $A=-L_\beta\geq0$ on $L^2(\beta)$ and $P_t=e^{-tA}$.
Assume that $\beta$ satisfies the ordinary log-Sobolev inequality with
constant $\lambda>0$, equivalently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-entropy-decay}
  \cH_\beta(P_t r)
  \leq e^{-2\lambda t}\cH_\beta(r).
\end{equation}
The Bakry--\'Emery criterion gives \eqref{eq:local-entropy-decay} whenever
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strong-log-concavity}
  \nabla^2V\succeq\lambda I_d.
\end{equation}
Thus strong log-concavity is a concrete sufficient condition at the local
level \citep{BakryGentilLedoux2014}.

\subsection{Bochner subordination}

Let $\phi$ be a Bernstein function with $\phi(0)=0$.  There is a convolution
semigroup of probability measures $(\eta_t)_{t\geq0}$ on $[0,\infty)$ such
that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subordinator-laplace}
  \int_0^\infty e^{-qs}\eta_t(\dd s)=e^{-t\phi(q)}.
\end{equation}
The subordinate Markov semigroup is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subordinate-semigroup}
  P_t^\phi r
  \coloneqq\int_0^\infty P_s r\,\eta_t(\dd s),
  \qquad
  P_t^\phi=e^{-t\phi(A)}.
\end{equation}
This is Bochner subordination; see
\citet{SchillingSongVondracek2012,SchillingWang2012}.

If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bernstein-representation}
  \phi(q)=bq+\int_0^\infty(1-e^{-qt})\nu_\phi(\dd t),
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subordinate-generator}
  -\phi(A)h
  =bL_\beta h
  +\int_0^\infty(P_t h-h)\nu_\phi(\dd t).
\end{equation}
When $b=0$, this is a pure-jump generator.  If
$P_t(x,\dd y)$ denotes the transition kernel of the Langevin semigroup, its
jump kernel is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subordinate-jump-kernel}
  K_\phi(x,\dd y)
  =\int_0^\infty P_t(x,\dd y)\nu_\phi(\dd t),
  \qquad y\neq x.
\end{equation}
Since every $P_t$ is reversible with respect to $\beta$, so is
$K_\phi$.  The corresponding entropy production is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subordinate-fisher}
  \cI_\phi(r)
  =\langle r,\phi(A)\log r\rangle_{L^2(\beta)}
  =-\left.\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\right|_{t=0^+}
       \cH_\beta(P_t^\phi r).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Subordination transfers MLSI]
\label{thm:subordination-pl}
Assume \eqref{eq:local-entropy-decay}.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subordinate-entropy-decay}
  \cH_\beta(P_t^\phi r)
  \leq e^{-t\phi(2\lambda)}\cH_\beta(r),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subordinate-pl}
  \cI_\phi(r)
  \geq\phi(2\lambda)\cH_\beta(r).
\end{equation}
Consequently the subordinate generator's MLSI constant satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subordinate-pl-constant}
  \kappa_\phi\geq\frac{\phi(2\lambda)}{2}.
\end{equation}
If $b=0$ and the logarithmic-mean metric for $K_\phi$ satisfies
\eqref{eq:slope-identity}, the same bound holds for its metric PL constant.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The map $u\mapsto u\log u$ is convex.  Jensen's inequality in
\eqref{eq:subordinate-semigroup}, followed by
\eqref{eq:local-entropy-decay}, yields
\begin{align*}
  \cH_\beta(P_t^\phi r)
  &\leq\int_0^\infty\cH_\beta(P_s r)\eta_t(\dd s)\\
  &\leq\cH_\beta(r)
      \int_0^\infty e^{-2\lambda s}\eta_t(\dd s)
   =e^{-t\phi(2\lambda)}\cH_\beta(r),
\end{align*}
where the last equality is \eqref{eq:subordinator-laplace}.
Differentiate at $t=0^+$ and use
\eqref{eq:subordinate-fisher}.  The normalization
$\cI_\phi\geq2\kappa_\phi\cH_\beta$ gives
\eqref{eq:subordinate-pl-constant}; when $b=0$, this is precisely
Definition~\ref{def:nonlocal-pl} for $K_\phi$.
\end{proof}

This argument requires only entropy contraction of the base semigroup, not
pointwise curvature.  Therefore any target satisfying a classical LSI can be
used, even when its LSI is proved by a method other than strong
log-concavity.

The lower bound has a useful spectral counterpart.  Let $\lambda_{\rm gap}$
be the optimal Poincar\'e constant of $A$.

\begin{proposition}[Subordinate MLSI bracket]
\label{prop:subordinate-mlsi-bracket}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:subordination-pl}, let
$\kappa_\phi$ denote the optimal constant in
$\cI_\phi\geq2\kappa_\phi\cH_\beta$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-subordinate-bracket}
  \frac{\phi(2\lambda)}2
  \leq \kappa_\phi
  \leq \phi(\lambda_{\rm gap}).
\end{equation}
When $b=0$, this is $\kappa_{\rm MLSI}(\beta,K_\phi)$ as defined in
\eqref{eq:optimal-mlsi-constant}; the full bracket also applies when
\eqref{eq:bernstein-representation} contains a local drift term $bA$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The lower bound is Theorem~\ref{thm:subordination-pl}.  By spectral calculus,
the optimal Poincar\'e constant of $\phi(A)$ is
$\phi(\lambda_{\rm gap})$, since $\phi$ is increasing and $\phi(0)=0$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:pl-implies-gap} says that an MLSI constant cannot
exceed this spectral gap.
\end{proof}

When $b>0$, the generator \eqref{eq:subordinate-generator} combines a local
diffusion with jumps, so its gradient structure is not a purely nonlocal
transport geometry.  The fractional powers $\phi(q)=q^{s/2}$ with
$0<s<2$ have $b=0$ and are genuinely pure jump.

\section{The Gaussian target}

\subsection{Fractional powers of the Ornstein--Uhlenbeck generator}

Take $\beta=\gamma_d=\mathcal N(0,I_d)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ou-generator}
  L_{\rm OU}=\Delta-x\cdot\nabla,
  \qquad A_{\rm OU}=-L_{\rm OU}.
\end{equation}
The Gaussian log-Sobolev constant is $\lambda=1$.  For a fractional order
$0<s<2$, choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fractional-ou-generator}
  \phi(q)=q^{s/2},
  \qquad
  L_s^{\rm OU}=-A_{\rm OU}^{s/2}.
\end{equation}
This is the fractional power of the \emph{Ornstein--Uhlenbeck generator},
not the operator obtained by replacing $\Delta$ with
$-(-\Delta)^{s/2}$ in \eqref{eq:ou-generator}.

The Mehler kernel is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mehler-kernel}
  P_t^{\rm OU}(x,\dd y)
  =\mathcal N\!\left(e^{-t}x,(1-e^{-2t})I_d\right)(\dd y).
\end{equation}
Since
\[
  q^{a}=\frac{a}{\Gamma(1-a)}
  \int_0^\infty(1-e^{-qt})\frac{\dd t}{t^{1+a}},
  \qquad a=s/2\in(0,1),
\]
the explicit Gaussian-reversible jump kernel is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fractional-ou-jump-kernel}
  K_s^{\rm OU}(x,\dd y)
  =\frac{s/2}{\Gamma(1-s/2)}
  \int_0^\infty
  \mathcal N\!\left(e^{-t}x,(1-e^{-2t})I_d\right)(\dd y)
  \frac{\dd t}{t^{1+s/2}},
  \qquad y\neq x.
\end{equation}
At short scales this is a singular fractional-type jump mechanism; globally
it is tilted by the Ornstein--Uhlenbeck dynamics so that the Gaussian remains
reversible.

\begin{corollary}[Gaussian fractional-OU MLSI]
\label{cor:gaussian-fractional-ou-pl}
For the generator \eqref{eq:fractional-ou-generator},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-fractional-ou-mlsi}
  \cI_s^{\rm OU}(r)
  \geq 2^{s/2}\cH_{\gamma_d}(r).
\end{equation}
Hence its MLSI constant satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-fractional-ou-pl-constant}
  \kappa_s^{\rm OU}\geq 2^{s/2-1}.
\end{equation}
Moreover the optimal constant obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-fractional-ou-bracket}
  2^{s/2-1}\leq\kappa_{s,\rm opt}^{\rm OU}\leq1.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The lower bound is Theorem~\ref{thm:subordination-pl} with
$\lambda=1$ and $\phi(q)=q^{s/2}$.  For the upper bound, use the Gaussian
translation density
\[
  r_m(x)=\exp(mx_1-m^2/2),
  \qquad r_m\gamma_d=\mathcal N(me_1,I_d).
\]
One has $\cH_{\gamma_d}(r_m)=m^2/2$.  Since constants are annihilated by
$A_{\rm OU}^{s/2}$ and $x_1$ is an eigenfunction with eigenvalue $1$,
\[
  \cI_s^{\rm OU}(r_m)
  =\langle r_m,A_{\rm OU}^{s/2}\log r_m\rangle_{L^2(\gamma_d)}
  =m\int x_1r_m\,\dd\gamma_d
  =m^2.
\]
Thus $\cI_s^{\rm OU}(r_m)/(2\cH_{\gamma_d}(r_m))=1$, which forces
$\kappa_{s,\rm opt}^{\rm OU}\leq1$.
\end{proof}

The bound from subordination is sharp at $s=2$, where the classical Gaussian
LSI gives $\kappa=1$.  For $0<s<2$, determining the sharp MLSI constant of
$A_{\rm OU}^{s/2}$ remains a separate problem; the simple Jensen argument
gives a dimension-free lower bound but need not be sharp.

\subsection{Direct stable-like kernels for stretched-exponential targets}

There is another natural construction, closer pointwise to the fractional
Laplacian.  Let $p=\dd\beta/\dd x$ and define the symmetric edge density
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetrized-stable-edge}
  j_{s,\beta}(x,y)
  =\frac{c_{d,s}}{2\abs{x-y}^{d+s}}\bigl(p(x)+p(y)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetrized-stable-kernel}
  K_{s,\beta}(x,\dd y)
  =\frac{j_{s,\beta}(x,y)}{p(x)}\,\dd y
  =\frac{c_{d,s}}{2\abs{x-y}^{d+s}}
   \left(1+\frac{p(y)}{p(x)}\right)\dd y
\end{equation}
is reversible with respect to $\beta$.  Its Dirichlet form agrees, up to the
factor $c_{d,s}/2$, with the stable-like form studied by
\citet{WangWang2015StableLike}:
\[
  \iint\frac{\abs{h(x)-h(y)}^2}{\abs{x-y}^{d+s}}
  \,\dd y\,e^{-V(x)}\dd x.
\]
Their estimates apply to a family substantially larger than the Gaussian.

\begin{proposition}[MLSI for stable-like stretched-exponential targets]
\label{prop:stable-like-stretched-exponential}
Fix $0<s<2$ and $q>0$, and let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stretched-exponential-target}
  \beta_q(\dd x)=Z_q^{-1}
    \exp\!\left(-(1+\abs x^2)^q\right)\dd x.
\end{equation}
For the reversible kernel \eqref{eq:symmetrized-stable-kernel}, there is a
constant $\kappa_{d,s,q}>0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stable-like-stretched-mlsi}
  \cI_{K_{s,\beta_q}}(r)
  \geq2\kappa_{d,s,q}\cH_{\beta_q}(r).
\end{equation}
If the metric slope identity \eqref{eq:slope-identity} is available for this
singular target-dependent kernel, the same estimate is the corresponding
nonlocal Wasserstein--PL inequality.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Corollary~1.5(2) of \citet{WangWang2015StableLike} gives a
super-Poincar\'e inequality for the stable-like form and every $q>0$, with a
profile that grows polynomially, up to a logarithmic factor, as its scale
parameter tends to zero.  Such a profile is bounded by
$\exp(C(1+r^{-1}))$ after adjusting $C$.  The standard equivalence between
this super-Poincar\'e bound and a Gross log-Sobolev inequality therefore gives
\eqref{eq:gross-jump-lsi} for some $\lambda_G>0$; see also the equivalence
recalled in the introduction of the cited paper.  Proposition
\ref{prop:gross-implies-modified} then proves
\eqref{eq:stable-like-stretched-mlsi}, after absorbing the fixed normalization
of \eqref{eq:symmetrized-stable-edge} into the constant.
\end{proof}

For $q\geq1/2$, the potential $(1+\abs x^2)^q$ is convex: its radial Hessian
eigenvalue is
$2q(1+\abs x^2)^{q-2}[1+(2q-1)\abs x^2]$, and its tangential eigenvalues are
positive.  Hence \eqref{eq:stretched-exponential-target} is log-concave.  For
$1/2\leq q<1$, its tails are slower than Gaussian and it does not satisfy the
ordinary Gross LSI for the local gradient Dirichlet form, since that
inequality implies Gaussian concentration \citep{BakryGentilLedoux2014}.
Proposition
\ref{prop:stable-like-stretched-exponential} shows that the stable-like jump
form nevertheless satisfies a Gross LSI and hence MLSI.  Long jumps can thus
restore entropy coercivity that is absent from the local Langevin geometry.
For $q\geq1$, the potential is in fact strongly convex, so both this direct
stable-like construction and the subordinate-Langevin construction apply.
The case $q=1$ is Gaussian up to a harmless rescaling.

For a Gaussian target, this construction and fractional OU subordination are
different.  Both are Gaussian-reversible and nonlocal, but their edge kernels
and spectra differ.  It is therefore misleading to ask for ``the''
fractional Gaussian MLSI, or metric PL, constant without fixing the generator.

The strongest robust statement is therefore not ``log-concavity implies a
fractional log-Sobolev inequality.''  It is:
\begin{quote}
Choose a reversible nonlocal generator together with its target.  Prove MLSI
for that pair, either directly, by curvature, by comparison, through a Gross
LSI, or by subordination of a semigroup whose entropy contraction is already
known.  To call the result a metric PL theorem, separately verify that the
chosen nonlocal action identifies $\cI_K$ with the squared entropy slope.
\end{quote}

Strong log-concavity is especially useful because it supplies the known base
entropy contraction needed by subordination.  Mere log-concavity can still be
enough for particular targets and kernels, but not by a target-only theorem
of the form suggested in the question.

\section{A practical theorem template for the book}

The following compact statement captures the part that can be integrated
safely into the treatment of nonlocal Wasserstein flows.

\begin{theorem}[Nonlocal entropy-dissipation and PL dictionary]
\label{thm:book-template}
Let $K$ be reversible with respect to a probability measure $\beta$ and
assume the entropy-dissipation identity \eqref{eq:entropy-dissipation}.  For
$r=\dd\alpha/\dd\beta$, the following statements are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item $(\beta,K)$ satisfies the modified log-Sobolev inequality
  $\cE_K(r,\log r)\geq2\kappa\Ent_\beta(r)$;
  \item the reversible jump semigroup satisfies
  $\KL((P_t r)\beta\mid\beta)
  \leq e^{-2\kappa t}\KL(r\beta\mid\beta)$.
\end{enumerate}
If, moreover, the logarithmic-mean distance \eqref{eq:nonlocal-distance}
satisfies \eqref{eq:slope-identity}, these are equivalent to the
$\cW_K$-PL inequality for $f(\alpha)=\KL(\alpha\mid\beta)$.  They then imply
$\cW_K^2(\alpha,\beta)\leq2\KL(\alpha\mid\beta)/\kappa$ on the finite-distance
component of $\beta$.  In all cases a necessary condition is the spectral-gap
inequality $\cE_K(h,h)\geq\kappa\Var_\beta(h)$.
\end{theorem}

The subordinate Langevin theorem can then be presented as the main positive
example: strong log-concavity of the target implies a local LSI, and
subordination converts it into a reversible fractional jump flow with an
explicit MLSI lower bound and, once \eqref{eq:slope-identity} is verified, the
same metric PL lower bound.

\section{Open questions and research directions}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=2.2em]
  \item \textbf{Sharp Gaussian constant.}
  Determine the exact MLSI constant of $(-L_{\rm OU})^{s/2}$.  This note gives
  $2^{s/2-1}\leq\kappa_{\rm opt}\leq1$.
  \item \textbf{Positive nonlocal curvature.}
  Find checkable assumptions on a nonhomogeneous singular kernel implying
  positive geodesic convexity of entropy in $\cW_K$.  Translation-invariant
  kernels give only zero curvature in the available theory.
  \item \textbf{Canonical kernels for a prescribed target.}
  Compare subordinate Langevin, Metropolis-adjusted stable, ground-state
  transformed, and symmetrized stable-like kernels.  They share the target
  but need not share MLSI constants or even the same finite-distance
  components.
  \item \textbf{Mere log-concavity.}
  Identify additional kernel lower bounds or Lyapunov conditions under which
  a log-concave target with zero curvature satisfies MLSI.  A dimension-free
  statement cannot depend on the target alone.
  \item \textbf{Sharp KL profiles.}
  Proposition~\ref{prop:weak-lsi-kl-profile} converts weak log-Sobolev
  profiles into power-KL bounds, but generally not sharply.  Determine the
  optimal desingularizer $\omega$ for concrete tail-degenerate kernels and
  decide when the resulting entropy rates are attained.
  \item \textbf{Metric rigor for singular target-dependent kernels.}
  For kernels such as \eqref{eq:fractional-ou-jump-kernel} and
  \eqref{eq:symmetrized-stable-kernel}, verify in one common framework the
  lower semicontinuity of the action, existence of geodesics, identification
  of the entropy slope, and convergence of minimizing movements.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Final answer}

For the reversible jump flow of
$f(\alpha)=\KL(\alpha\mid\beta)$, the correct entropy-production condition is
\[
  \boxed{
  \frac12\iint
  (r(y)-r(x))(\log r(y)-\log r(x))
  \,\beta(\dd x)K(x,\dd y)
  \geq2\kappa\int r\log r\,\dd\beta,
  \qquad r=\frac{\dd\alpha}{\dd\beta}.}
\]
It is exactly the modified log-Sobolev inequality of the reversible jump
generator and yields entropy decay $e^{-2\kappa t}$.  It is also exactly the
nonlocal Wasserstein--PL inequality when the logarithmic-mean action
\eqref{eq:nonlocal-action} induces a metric satisfying the slope identity
\eqref{eq:slope-identity}; this extra metric identification should not be
suppressed for singular target-dependent kernels.

This inequality is not implied by log-concavity, or even Gaussianity, of
$\beta$ without assumptions on $K$.  It \emph{is} guaranteed for a clean
fractional model when one starts from a target satisfying an ordinary LSI and
subordinates its reversible Langevin semigroup.  For
$\beta=\mathcal N(0,I_d)$ and generator
$-(-L_{\rm OU})^{s/2}$, one obtains the explicit Gaussian-reversible jump
kernel \eqref{eq:fractional-ou-jump-kernel} and the quantitative bound
\[
  \boxed{\kappa_s\geq2^{s/2-1}.}
\]
The pure translation-invariant fractional Laplacian, by contrast, has no
Gaussian reversible probability target and should not be used to formulate
this particular PL question without first modifying the generator.
