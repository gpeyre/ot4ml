## Original findings (resolved)

### CH5-001 - Moderate - The Brenier proof assumes an unstated noncompact dual-attainment and extended-domain theorem

**Lines/environment.** Lines 154-165,
`prop-kantorovich-duality-general`; 317-330, `def-c-transform`; 438-460,
`def-legendre-fenchel-transform` and the quadratic conversion; 464-490,
`rem-proof-brenier`. Imported formal theorem/proof:
`monge.tex:658-684`, `thm-brenier`.

**Precise claim.** The proved dual theorem assumes compact metric `X,Y` and
finite continuous `c`, and obtains a maximum over `C(X) x C(Y)`. Line 164
only gestures at unspecified l.s.c./integrability extensions and warns that a
maximum may become a supremum. The remark titled "Proof of Brenier's theorem"
then takes arbitrary `alpha,beta in P_2(R^d)`, says "let `(u,v)` be a
closed optimal dual pair," uses both pointwise transform equalities over full
Euclidean space, and invokes support contact. The earlier formal proof of
Brenier likewise tells the reader to choose optimal c-concave potentials and
delegates that step to this chapter. Neither location states the optimizer
theorem, its integrability/value class, or the meaning of "closed" here.

**Adversarial scope test.** Compactness is used twice in lines 179-215: to
close/extract in the separating cone and to obtain a uniformly bounded
equicontinuous maximizing sequence by Arzela--Ascoli. Those steps do not apply
verbatim on `R^d`.

For an exact function-space counterexample, take

```text
alpha = Uniform(0,1),  beta = N(0,1),  c(x,y)=(x-y)^2/2.
```

The optimal map is `T(x)=Phi^{-1}(x)`. A globally finite convex
`phi=x^2/2-u` on `R` cannot satisfy
`T(x) in partial phi(x)` a.e. on `(0,1)`: a finite convex function is
locally Lipschitz around the interior point 1, so its nearby subgradients are
bounded, whereas `Phi^{-1}(x)->+infinity` as `x->1-`.

This does not disprove Brenier or dual attainment. Up to a constant, set

```text
phi(x)=integral_(1/2)^x Phi^{-1}(s) ds on [0,1],
phi(x)=+infinity outside [0,1].
```

The endpoint integrals are finite; this is proper l.s.c. convex and has
`partial phi(x)={Phi^{-1}(x)}` on `(0,1)`. Its conjugate is finite on
`R`, while `u=x^2/2-phi` is `-infinity` off its effective domain. Thus
the natural solution exists but lies outside the real-valued input class of
lines 319/324.

For quadratic `P_2` data, `0<=c(x,y)<=|x|^2+|y|^2` gives integrable
separable domination. A standard noncompact dual-attainment result can therefore
supply an extended conjugate pair; alternatively, primal existence plus
c-cyclical monotonicity yields a proper closed convex potential directly.
Either route is a theorem absent from this derivation. Contact is initially
`pi`-a.e.; support contact then needs continuity of finite slack or
closedness of the Fenchel equality set.

**Smallest repair.** Before line 466, state and cite the exact quadratic
noncompact attainment result, including integrable/extended potentials,
ambient versus marginal-support domains, transform closure, and a.e. contact.
Alternatively, construct `phi` from the c-cyclically monotone support of an
optimal plan. Define "closed pair" and permit `phi=+infinity` and
`u=-infinity` off effective domains. A shorter repair is to restrict the
remark to compactly supported marginals and explicitly defer full `P_2`
Brenier.

**Impact.** Brenier's theorem and all quadratic conjugacy signs remain true.
The missing bridge affects the advertised proof here and the earlier formal
proof that relies on it, but not compact duality or compact finite-envelope
models. This supports Moderate, not Major.

### CH5-002 - Moderate - The 1D map/derivative sentence omits atomlessness and differentiability qualifiers

**Lines/environment.** Lines 248-254, especially line 251, after
`prop-continuous-complementary-slackness`. Imported definitions/results:
`monge.tex:1229-1242`, 1254-1275, and 1282-1305.

**Precise claim.** The text unconditionally writes
`T=F_beta^{-1} o F_alpha` and says on the active graph
`f'(x)=2(x-T(x))`, without source atomlessness, generalized-inverse
convention, or an a.e./differentiability qualifier.

**Counterexamples and derivation.** If
`alpha=delta_0` and `beta=(delta_{-1}+delta_1)/2`, every deterministic
push-forward of `alpha` is a single Dirac; no map induces the quantile
coupling. If instead `alpha=Uniform[0,1]` and
`beta=(delta_0+delta_1)/2`, the monotone map exists but is a step function.
At its jump the corresponding convex potential has a corner, so an ordinary
derivative need not exist.

At a differentiability/contact point, the envelope identity for
`c(x,y)=(x-y)^2` gives
`f'(x)=partial_x c(x,T(x))=2(x-T(x))`. Otherwise one needs the
super/subdifferential relation. In 1D a semiconcave potential has at most
countably many nondifferentiability points, so an atomless source makes the
formula true `alpha`-a.e. Target atomlessness is unnecessary. The adjacent
generators use smooth positive sampled densities, but line 251 is general
prose.

**Smallest repair.** State that if `alpha` is atomless, then using the
generalized inverse from `def-cdf-quantile`,
`T=F_beta^{-1} o F_alpha` is the monotone optimal map `alpha`-a.e.; the
derivative identity holds wherever `f` is differentiable, hence
`alpha`-a.e. here. Give the sub/superdifferential contact at exceptional
points.

**Impact.** Atomic/mixed applications and the imported logical chain are
affected. The smooth figures are not.

### CH5-003 - Moderate - Restricted biconjugation in the hard-transform figure is not the ordinary concave majorant

**Lines/environment.** Lines 494-550, especially line 550; figure
`fig:dual-alternating-c-transform-failure`, lines 552-564. Generator:
`notebooks-figures/dual-alternating-c-transform-failure.ipynb`.

**Precise claim.** For `c(x,y)=-xy`, the text correctly first identifies
`f^{c bar c}` with a concave majorant restricted to slopes from the opposite
domain, then falsely says the displayed interval contains every relevant
supporting slope, so the restricted closure equals the ordinary concave
envelope.

**Derivation/diagnostic.** Since
`f^c(y)=inf_x{-xy-f(x)}`, supporting affine sections have slope `-y`.
With `X=Y=[-1.25,1.25]`, only slopes in `-Y=[-1.25,1.25]` are available.
An independent upper-hull calculation on the exact 900-point notebook arrays
gave:

| Input | Ordinary hull slope range | Allowed `-Y` | Max restricted-minus-ordinary |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `f` | `[-2.7351372678,1.7961902161]` | `[-1.25,1.25]` | `0.2711609203` |
| `g` | `[-1.9668828870,1.1562346147]` | `[-1.25,1.25]` | `0.0187323126` |

The mismatch is not tolerance. Conversely, the restricted triple-transform
residual is `2.220446049e-16`, confirming the stated idempotence.

**Smallest repair.** Call the red curves restricted concave closures generated
by slopes in `-Y` and delete the ordinary-envelope assertion. If ordinary
majorants are required, enlarge the opposite grids past the measured ranges
(for example to at least `[-2.8,2.8]`) and regenerate both PDFs.

**Impact.** Only the geometric/numerical identification is wrong. Sequential
feasibility, monotonicity, exact hard closure, and the Sinkhorn/auction
distinction remain correct.

### CH5-007 - Minor - Properness alone does not keep the stated biconjugation identities inside the declared value class

**Lines/environment.** Lines 438-447,
`def-legendre-fenchel-transform`, and line 503.

**Precise claim.** Inputs are declared as
`h:R^d -> R union {+infinity}`. Line 503 says `f***=f*` and `f**` is
the l.s.c. convex envelope of a proper `f`, without an affine-minorant
condition or an improper `-infinity` convention.

**Counterexample.** For the proper finite function `f(x)=-x^2`,

```text
f*(y)=sup_x(xy+x^2)=+infinity for every y,
f**(x)=-infinity for every x.
```

Thus `f**` is not a proper l.s.c. convex envelope, and forming `f***`
requires conjugating a `-infinity`-valued input excluded by line 441. A fully
extended convention can retain a possibly improper identity, but it is not
stated.

**Smallest repair.** Require that `f` have an affine minorant, equivalently
that `f*` be proper, before calling `f**` the proper closed convex
envelope and using `f***`. Alternatively, explicitly admit improper
`+/-infinity` functions and say "possibly improper envelope."

**Impact.** Local endpoint precision only. The finite compact c-transform
algebra in Proposition `prop-c-transform-algebra` is unaffected.

## RQ5 register (excluded from totals)

### RQ5-001 - Add the contact-set/c-cyclical-monotonicity bridge?

For contact pairs and every permutation `sigma`,

```text
sum_i c(x_i,y_i)
 = sum_i f(x_i)+sum_i g(y_i)
 = sum_i f(x_i)+sum_i g(y_{sigma(i)})
 <= sum_i c(x_i,y_{sigma(i)}).
```

Thus every dual contact set is c-cyclically monotone. Adding this after
continuous complementary slackness would connect directly to
`kantorovich.tex:1412` onward and to the robust Brenier repair. Its absence is
not a false claim.
